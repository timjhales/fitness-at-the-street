# Fitness At The Street — New Member UX Wireframe Brief

Create a **low-to-mid fidelity UX wireframe/prototype for the Fitness At The Street community gym website**.

## Purpose

This is **not a finished visual redesign**.

The purpose of this prototype is to show the owners, Steve and Kim, how the website could be structured around the **new-member journey**, so they can approve or challenge:

* whether the content is relevant
* whether anything important is missing
* whether the gym's services are represented accurately
* whether the joining journey reflects the real experience
* whether the balance between gym, personal training, classes, Trainerize, community and the gym's heritage feels right

Do **not** invent operational details that have not been supplied. Where the actual process is unknown, use clearly labelled placeholders such as `[CONFIRM PROCESS]`.

---

## Core UX objective

The website should answer four questions for a prospective new member:

1. **Is this gym for me?**
2. **What do I get as a member?**
3. **What happens when I join?**
4. **How do I get started?**

The site should feel welcoming rather than intimidating and should make the first visit and joining process feel straightforward.

The central proposition should be:

> **A welcoming, strength-focused community gym where people can train their way, get the support they need and make real progress.**

Do not make the site feel like a hardcore bodybuilding gym.

Do not make it feel like a generic commercial gym.

The community, support and progression are as important as strength.

---

## Proposed navigation

Create a simple desktop navigation:

**Home | Start Here | Training | Our Story | See Inside | Find Us | JOIN**

Include **Member Login** as a smaller secondary utility link.

Under **Training**, show:

* Gym
* Personal Training
* Classes
* Training Programmes
* Trainerize

Under **Our Story**, show:

* About
* Meet The Team
* Snowling Stones

The primary CTA throughout the site should be **JOIN** or **START HERE**, not Member Login.

---

## Homepage wireframe

Build the homepage as a long-scroll desktop layout.

### 1. Header

Show:

* Fitness At The Street logo placeholder
* Main navigation
* Prominent JOIN button
* Smaller Member Login link

Keep the header simple and uncluttered.

---

### 2. Hero

Headline:

**Train Hard. Feel Strong. Belong.**

Supporting text:

**A strength-focused community gym in Brundall where you can train your way.**

Primary CTA:

**START HERE**

Secondary CTA:

**SEE INSIDE** *(links to the Matterport 3D tour)*

Use a large real-gym photography placeholder rather than a generic fitness image.

Add a small annotation:

> Primary job of hero: immediately establish what the gym is, who it is for and what a new visitor should do next.

---

### 3. "Find your way to train"

Headline:

**Find your way to train**

Four equal cards:

**GYM**
Train independently in a strength-focused community environment.

**PERSONAL TRAINING**
One-to-one support, coaching and accountability.

**PROGRAMMES + TRAINERIZE**
Individual training programmes, workouts and progress tracking through the Trainerize app.

**CLASSES**
Structured group training and the social side of the gym.

Each card should have:

* image placeholder
* short description
* Learn More link

The four options should feel complementary rather than competing products.

Add annotation:

> These are four ways a member can engage with Fitness At The Street. Confirm whether this accurately represents the actual member experience.

---

### 4. "Is Fitness At The Street for you?"

Headline:

**Is Fitness At The Street for you?**

Three cards:

**I'M NEW TO TRAINING**
You don't need to know what you're doing before you arrive.

**I'M GETTING BACK INTO EXERCISE**
Start at your own pace with support from the team.

**I ALREADY TRAIN**
Train independently, follow a programme, attend classes or work with a trainer.

Include a fourth smaller option:

**I'M NOT SURE**
Talk to the team and we'll help you find the right starting point.

This section is designed to reduce the intimidation barrier for new members.

---

### 5. "What happens when you join?"

This is one of the most important sections.

Headline:

**What happens when you join?**

Show a clear five-step horizontal journey:

**01 — GET IN TOUCH**
Tell us what you're looking for.

**02 — MEET THE TEAM**
Talk through your goals and experience.

**03 — GET YOUR PROGRAMME**
Get training appropriate to your goals and ability.

**04 — GET STARTED WITH TRAINERIZE**
Access your workouts through the app.

**05 — TRAIN & PROGRESS**
Train, track your workouts and see your progress.

Where any step is not confirmed, label it:

**[CONFIRM ACTUAL PROCESS]**

Do not invent inductions, assessments, consultations or other procedures.

Add a prominent link:

**What to expect on your first visit →**

---

### 6. "Your training goes beyond the gym"

Headline:

**Your training goes beyond the gym**

Explain Trainerize in terms of the member benefit, not the technology.

Supporting copy:

> Your membership can include an individual training programme delivered through the Trainerize app, helping you know what to do, track your workouts and see your progress over time.

Use three phone/app screenshot placeholders:

**YOUR PROGRAMME**
Your individual training plan.

**YOUR WORKOUTS**
Know what you're doing when you train.

**YOUR PROGRESS**
Record sessions and track improvement.

Add a small label:

**Powered by Trainerize**

Do not make Trainerize the headline or make the section feel like an advert for the software.

Add annotation:

> Trainerize is part of the member experience, not a standalone technology feature.

---

### 7. Personal Training section

Headline:

**Need more individual support?**

Create a strong personal training section.

Show:

* trainer photography placeholder
* short description
* benefits
* CTA

Suggested copy:

> Work one-to-one with a trainer for focused coaching, support and accountability.

Potential benefits:

* Build confidence
* Improve technique
* Work towards specific goals
* Get individual guidance
* Stay accountable

CTA:

**EXPLORE PERSONAL TRAINING**

Do not make personal training feel like an optional afterthought.

Add annotation:

> Personal training is a core route into the Fitness At The Street experience and should be treated as such.

---

### 8. Community / testimonials

Headline:

**A community built around strength**

Use 3-4 testimonial placeholders.

Do not simply display generic five-star reviews.

Select testimonials that answer new-member concerns such as:

* "I was nervous about joining"
* "I didn't know what I was doing"
* "The trainers are supportive"
* "I finally stuck with training"
* "It feels like a community"

Include a small Google review/ratings element if appropriate.

The objective is **reassurance**, not simply social proof.

---

### 9. "Strength is part of our story"

This is where the Snowling Stones story belongs.

Keep this section visually **smaller than the main member proposition**.

Headline:

**Strength is part of our story**

Supporting copy:

> Fitness At The Street has its roots in strength and traditional stone lifting. Steve's experience travelling around the country lifting stones, including the famous Dinnie Stones, helped shape the ethos behind the gym.

Then:

> That heritage is part of the gym's identity today, but strength here is about more than lifting the heaviest weight. It's about learning, progressing, challenging yourself and supporting the people around you.

Use a real Steve stone-lifting photograph placeholder.

CTA:

**DISCOVER THE SNOWLING STONES**

Important:

Do **not** make the stones the main homepage proposition.

Add annotation:

> Snowling Stones = heritage and authenticity, not the primary product proposition.

---

### 10. See inside the gym

Headline:

**See inside the gym**

Use a strip/grid of real photography placeholders showing:

* gym floor
* equipment
* people training
* trainers helping members
* classes
* entrance

Include a placeholder for an embedded **Matterport 3D tour** (not a video), shown as a bordered "Matterport viewer" box with a play/navigate icon, visually distinct from the photo grid.

CTA:

**TAKE THE TOUR** *(opens/launches the Matterport tour)*

The photography should show real people and the actual environment wherever possible.

Add annotation:

> Tour = embedded Matterport 3D walkthrough, not a produced video. Wireframe should show this as a distinct interactive viewer element, not a standard image or video placeholder.

---

### 11. Final conversion section

Headline:

**Ready to get started?**

Supporting text:

> Whether you're new to training, returning after a break or already experienced, we'll help you find the right way to train.

Primary CTA:

**JOIN**

Secondary CTA:

**ASK US A QUESTION**

---

### 12. Footer

Include:

* contact details
* opening hours `[CONFIRM]`
* address
* Find Us
* social links
* Member Login
* Privacy
* Terms
* Trainerize/member access if appropriate

---

## Visual direction

This is a **wireframe**, not a finished website.

Use:

* neutral/greyscale palette
* simple typography
* clear content hierarchy
* obvious image placeholders
* cards and content blocks
* arrows/flow indicators where useful
* generous whitespace
* restrained UI

Do not spend time on:

* elaborate colour schemes
* decorative graphics
* gradients
* animations
* elaborate photography treatment
* highly polished branding

The objective is to make the **information architecture and member journey obvious**.

---

## Approval layer

At the top of the prototype, include a small banner:

**FITNESS AT THE STREET — NEW MEMBER UX CONCEPT**

**Purpose: CONTENT & JOURNEY APPROVAL**

Then include a short note:

> This prototype is intended to confirm that the proposed member journey, services and messaging accurately reflect the Fitness At The Street experience before detailed design and development.

At the end of the prototype include an **"Approval / Questions"** section with these questions:

### PLEASE CONFIRM

1. **Does this accurately represent what a new member experiences?**
2. **Are Gym, Personal Training, Classes and Training Programmes the right core routes?**
3. **Is the Trainerize description accurate?**
4. **Is the joining process accurate?**
5. **What have we missed?**
6. **Is the balance between community, strength, coaching and heritage right?**
7. **Does the Snowling Stones story feel appropriately positioned?**

Make the prototype feel like a **UX workshop document for Steve and Kim**, rather than a finished marketing website.

The most important thing is that they can look at it and say:

> **"Yes, that's actually what we offer and that's how we want new members to experience us."**

Only after that approval should the detailed visual design and copywriting be developed.
