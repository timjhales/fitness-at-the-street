Fitness At The Street — New Member UX Concept

Purpose: Content & Journey Approval

This prototype is intended to confirm that the proposed member journey, services and messaging accurately reflect the Fitness At The Street experience before detailed design and development.

Start here

The homepage stays a community hub — it doesn't double as an onboarding funnel too

The original brief restructured the homepage itself around a new-member journey. But this site already serves an existing community — members checking the schedule, logging into Trainerize, finding contact info. Turning the whole homepage into a "JOIN"-driven funnel risks burying what regulars actually come back for. So this concept keeps the existing homepage close to as-is, and builds the journey as its own dedicated page instead.

Part A — Homepage (existing, stays as-is)

Hero, community/reviews, "Follow Our Journey" — unchanged. The one addition: a prominent "New Here? Start Here" entry point near the top, for anyone landing there who isn't a member yet.

Part B — New page: /start-here

The full new-member journey from the brief lives here instead — answering Is this for me? / What do I get? / What happens when I join? / How do I get started? Reached via the homepage banner, a new nav item, and any acquisition marketing (ads, bio links, QR codes at the gym).

How to use this document: look at the live dev site first (ducks-new-build.fitnessatthestreet.pages.dev), then use the four tabs above — Main Site for what already exists and why, Start Here for the new journey page in full detail, Overview Diagram if you just want the shape of the idea without reading everything, and Things We Need To Cover Off for the full checklist of open questions and every image that needs reviewing. Every section carries a reviewer note explaining why it's structured that way, and a confirmation flag anywhere we're not certain we've got the real detail right. Nothing here is final — it's a starting point for a conversation about the logic, not a design.

Once we've talked through the reasoning together and agreed the structure — including whether this parallel approach is the right call — the next step is a higher-fidelity wireframe prototype, and only after that, the real build.

Existing site — under construction too

Why each current page exists (and what's still missing)

Before deciding how the new split-journey concept fits in, it's worth being straight about the site as it stands today — it's still under construction, built page-by-page against what Kim and Steve originally asked for. Here's the original list, whether each one actually exists yet, and how it sits once the new journey page is added.

Virtual Tour Approach proven, FATS scan not yet done

Why it exists

Let people see the gym before they visit — reduces the intimidation of walking in somewhere unfamiliar.

How the original ask changed

Kim and Steve originally asked for a "book a tour" option — a guided walkthrough, either professionally shot or phone + gimbal with commentary. Matterport's free tier makes a full self-guided 3D scan achievable instead, which would do the same job better: available any time, no need to schedule a person to film or guide it.

What's actually live right now

The embed currently on the Take The Tour page is a sample from another gym, used to prove the concept and test the layout — not FATS's own space. A real scan of the FATS gym floor still needs to be generated before this is genuinely built.

How it fits the new design

Matterport viewer featured distinctly in "See Inside The Gym" (Section 9) on /start-here — same caveat applies there, it's a concept placeholder until the real scan exists.

Equipment (general / powerlifter / bodybuilder tabs) Superseded — depth scales by persona, not a page

Why it exists

Reassure people training in a specific style that the gym actually has the equipment for it, rather than a generic once-over.

Refined plan

Not a page, not gated behind one persona either — equipment reassurance appears for everyone on the New Here Router (Section 2), but the depth scales: light and reassuring for a first-timer, fully technical for the Experienced Lifter path (specific kit, platforms, calibrated plates). No separate destination to build or maintain, and nobody sees detail that isn't meant for them. Tagging equipment inside a future FATS Matterport scan (see Virtual Tour above) could add extra proof later, but isn't required for this to work.

How it fits the new design

Woven through every persona's version of the Start Here page at the appropriate depth — see Section 5 (Trainerize) for where the Experienced Lifter's full-detail version sits.

Build trust and a human connection with the actual trainers before someone commits to joining or booking PT.

How it fits the new design

Unchanged as its own page (Instructors). Referenced from "Meet The Team" (Step 02 of the joining journey) and the Personal Training section (Section 6) on /start-here, rather than only being reachable via nav.

Upcoming Events & Socials Built — by design

Why it exists

Show the community/social side of the gym is real and active.

Clarification

The homepage "Follow Our Journey" wall using real past content (events, classes, members) rather than a live "upcoming" listing is a deliberate simulation of ongoing activity, not an oversight — corrected from how this was described in the previous version of this audit.

How it fits the new design

Social wall stays on the homepage (Part A below) — it's an existing-community feature, not a new-member conversion tool, so it doesn't belong on /start-here.

Snowling Stones Built

Why it exists

Tell the heritage/authenticity story behind the gym's identity.

How it fits the new design

Unchanged as its own page. Deliberately kept smaller and secondary within the journey (Section 8) — heritage supports the proposition, it isn't the proposition, per the original brief.

Why the split-journey approach is better than growing this list further The original approach was a flat list of standalone pages, each answering its own "what/where" question. Every new addition meant deciding from scratch whether it needed its own page and where it lived in nav — the Equipment question above is a live example of exactly that decision. The split-journey approach gives every future addition a clear home instead: does it help persuade a prospective member (it belongs on /start-here), or does it serve people who are already members (it belongs on the main site)? That's the actual justification for restructuring, beyond just the homepage question raised earlier.
